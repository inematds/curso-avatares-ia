Write a short, easy-to-say line

You can rehearse a 5-to-15-second message without rushing.

Reading: about 15 minutes. Practice: 10 active minutes. Avatar creation, queues, and extra production need separate sessions.

An avatar needs a line a person could actually say. A paragraph full of instructions will not fit a short video.

In 1 minute

  1. Purpose: explain one decision
  2. Text: one or two short sentences
  3. Rehearsal: measure the audio duration

1One line, one purpose

Choose one purpose: introduce, explain a step, answer a question, or invite people to continue.

Use short sentences with words you would really say.

Write numbers the way you want them spoken.

Read names aloud to catch unclear pronunciations.

For Oficina Clara: Today we’ll choose a paintbrush.

Look at the shape of the tip before you begin.

This line guides one simple action.

It does not need to tell the workshop’s story or promise a result.

Bia writes performance directions outside the text the voice will read.

2The pause is part of the message

Record in your phone’s Voice Recorder app. Listen and check the time shown in the recording app.

If it runs longer than fifteen seconds, cut one idea.

If it is too short, leave a natural pause instead of filling it with adjectives.

Keep spoken text separate from performance directions; those belong in another field.

A small smile is a performance direction, not a phrase for the avatar to say.

If you put directions in the spoken text, the voice may read them aloud.

Review the audio before spending on animation.

Prepare your own voice for cloning

You can record and evaluate your own sample for a voice clone.

Reading: about 15 minutes. Practice: 10 active minutes. Avatar creation, queues, and extra production need separate sessions.

Cloning a voice does not fix a poor recording or remove service rules. Start with the source and sample quality.

In 1 minute

  1. Source: the learner’s own voice
  2. Recording: no music and a steady distance
  3. Comparison: a new sentence, not the reference

1A voice clone approximates vocal traits

Cloning uses a recording to approximate tone, rhythm, and pronunciation.

Record your own voice in a quiet place, with no music or other people talking.

Keep a steady distance from the microphone and avoid clipped sounds.

Listen carefully before you submit it.

If the voice crackles or sounds harsh on louder parts, move the microphone a little farther away or speak more softly.

Record it again and compare.

On ElevenLabs, instant voice cloning lets you record or upload audio and confirm your rights to the voice.

Professional voice cloning has a separate verification process for the voice owner.

Do not treat the two processes as equivalent.

For this exercise, use only your own voice.

Bia asks for a new sentence to compare the clone with the reference.

2Check the resemblance without confusing accent with identity

After creating a clone, use a sentence that is different from the reference recording.

Check whether the voice sounds recognizable, clear, and natural.

A change in accent or emotion can reduce the resemblance.

Keep the original audio so you can make a direct comparison.

Other services, such as MiniMax, also offer voice cloning.

Check that service’s own requirements and controls; steps from one service do not automatically apply to another.

The simplest alternative is still to record your own narration, when that works for the video.

Otávio records his own voice and checks that the last word was not cut off.

3Check the decision against its use

A recorded sample, a created clone, and synthesized speech are three different deliverables.
